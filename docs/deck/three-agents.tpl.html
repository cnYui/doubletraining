Three agents, one display

DoubleTraining logs your sets, DoubleRunner holds your cadence, WorkHealthier watches how you sit. Three separate projects, built on one runtime, one 480 × 352 single-green display, and one pair of temple gestures.

The shared thesis

Each one does something a phone in your pocket structurally can’t

The glasses already answer questions out loud. All three agents were scoped to the things a general voice assistant cannot do: remember your numbers, read the sensor already strapped to your head, and watch the thing you can’t see — yourself.

DTMemory

Every tap is a logged set, kept on the glasses. The next session starts from real numbers instead of a guess, and the summary can compare today with the last time you trained this muscle group.

DRA sensor already on your head

Running cadence usually needs a watch or a foot pod. Head-mounted, every stride arrives as a nod on the gyroscope, so the glasses can read cadence with no extra hardware and beat time against it.

180 spm detected within ±3 spm
60 Hz gyroscope, 6 s of curve on screen

WHA view of yourself

You cannot see your own head angle or how close you have drifted to the monitor. The glasses sit exactly where that measurement has to be taken, and say something only when it has been wrong for long enough to matter.

head down ≥ 18° held 8 s → alert
under 45 cm held 15 s → alert

No backend, no network, no companion app. All three run entirely on the glasses: pure logic modules that take now as an argument, state in localStorage, and a chat card fallback when the Page is shown inside the conversation instead of on the lens.

#40ff5e and nothing else. Hierarchy comes from luminance, size and position, never from a second hue, because the display has none — which is why every state also carries a shape: an outlined box, a double line, a filled box, a dashed alert frame.

Below 240 px of height the same Page becomes a card. DoubleTraining keeps the date, the count and the focus; DoubleRunner keeps the state, the cadence, the target and the hint; WorkHealthier keeps the status line and the two tiles. One layout, two densities — not a second Page.

Six findings that changed the products, not just the code

What the platform refused to do

No vibration API anywhere in AIUI 0.17

“Buzz the temple at 180 spm” was the obvious design and is not buildable: no haptic entry in the docs, the samples or the runtime.

DR · the beat is a 1200 Hz tick with an accent every fourth, plus a dot lit for 110 ms. In traffic the dot carries it alone.
No time-of-flight, laser or infrared

One 12 MP camera and an IMU. Distance has to come out of an image, and an image needs something of known size in it.

WH · a printed QR marker on the monitor, ranged by apparent size, with a one-tap calibration that cancels the printing error.
A double tap never reaches a Page

Confirmed with Page-level key logs: only the system home page reacts. The gesture is real on the glasses but untestable off them.

All three · every “back” is left to the host, and no destructive action hangs off a double tap.
new Date(y, m, d) could not be trusted

The same call returned different results between runs, and a chat card once opened 20 days ahead because a UTC offset in seconds was read as minutes.

DT · integer days since 1970-01-01 for all date maths, one normaliser for the offset, “today” only from Date.now().
The template engine lost rows and ignored classes

A dynamic class on the <page> root did nothing (black screen), loop variables were unbound on the element carrying ink:for, and rows inside a re-created ink:if never came back.

All three · static root class, every loop wrapped in a <block>, panels stay mounted and switch on a data-bound class.
A hidden browser pane renders zero frames

With the pane out of sight the runtime reports hidden and stops animating, so an import stalls mid-unpack and the preview freezes.

Process · Studio stays in front for every capture. Every screen in this deck was taken that way.
